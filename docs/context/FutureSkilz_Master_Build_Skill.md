# 4A. Standard Product, Consulting and Learning Instruction

Apply this standard to every FutureSkilz workflow, diagnostic, recommendation, Skill Shot and explanation.

## Management consulting standard

1. **Start with the decision.** State what the learner is trying to achieve, what decision the workflow supports and what evidence is needed.
2. **Structure the problem MECE.** Separate context, aspiration, capability and constraints. Within capability, distinguish human, human-to-human, self, AI, digital, functional and domain intelligence. Avoid duplicate questions or gaps between categories.
3. **Move from hypothesis to evidence.** Treat role titles and self-ratings as hypotheses. Ask for a recent situation, observable behaviour, outcome or work sample before drawing a conclusion.
4. **Drill down selectively.** Ask a follow-up only when the answer changes the diagnosis, target proficiency or recommended path. Do not make every learner complete every branch.
5. **Prioritise by value and feasibility.** Rank gaps using goal relevance, proficiency gap, urgency, prerequisite value, evidence confidence and time available.
6. **Make recommendations answer-first.** Present the conclusion, supporting evidence, implications and next action in that order.
7. **Show confidence and limitations.** Distinguish measured evidence, self-report, inference and assumptions. Never present simulated precision as validated assessment science.
8. **Make outputs mutually actionable.** Every identified priority must connect to a skill, target behaviour, learning intervention, practice task and evidence signal.

## Feynman learning standard

1. Explain each concept in plain language before introducing specialist terminology.
2. Use one concrete workplace example and one non-example to expose the boundary of the idea.
3. Ask the learner to explain or apply the idea in their own words and context.
4. Identify where their explanation becomes vague; use that point for the next question or practice task.
5. End with a short teach-back, observable action or decision that demonstrates understanding.
6. Prefer short sentences, specific verbs and familiar words. Define every necessary technical term at first use.

## Conversational diagnostic standard

- Open with a human, low-effort question and explain why the next question matters.
- Use progressive disclosure: one decision at a time, with click-through choices before optional free text.
- Cover the full capability system through structured branches rather than one long generic questionnaire.
- Ask about both strengths and friction. Diagnosis must not be deficit-only.
- Capture current role, desired outcome, time horizon, priority capability families, specific skill signals, recent challenge, confidence and learning constraints.
- Reflect important answers back in subsequent prompts so the conversation feels attentive rather than scripted.
- Keep the shortest valid path short; allow deeper diagnosis for learners who want greater precision.
- At completion, explain: what we heard, what it may mean, what remains uncertain and what happens next.

## Quality gate

Before shipping a workflow or recommendation, verify that it is:

- **MECE:** no material overlap or missing category.
- **Evidence-aware:** conclusions are traceable to learner input or demonstrated behaviour.
- **Comprehensive but progressive:** broad coverage without exposing unnecessary complexity at once.
- **Plain-language:** understandable without prior specialist knowledge.
- **Decision-useful:** changes prioritisation, action or evidence collection.
- **Connected:** maps to the Skill Graph and the end-to-end capability flywheel.

A Skill Shot is an approximately 10-minute focused learning experience.

Every Skill Shot includes:

- Title
- Target learner
- Skill
- Sub-skill
- Proficiency level
- Learning objective
- Why it matters
- Real-world context
- Core concept
- Framework/model
- Example
- Common mistake
- Practice
- AI interaction
- Knowledge check
- Reflection
- Evidence
- Mastery criteria
- Next step
- Sources/provenance
- Contributor attribution

Default flow:

0:00–1:00 — Why  
1:00–3:00 — Concept  
3:00–6:00 — Example  
6:00–8:00 — Practice  
8:00–9:00 — AI Coach  
9:00–10:00 — Demonstration

Do not force every Skill Shot into video. The agent chooses the most suitable format:

- Voice-over presentation
- Video
- Interactive lesson
- Case
- Scenario
- Simulation
- AI conversation

>

>
> Use an agentic orchestration architecture with learner agents, content factory agents and marketplace agents. Agents must be observable, governed and modular.
>
> Build configurable human approval gates for sensitive, regulated, low-confidence or high-risk content.
