# Knowledge check: Find the cause, not just the symptom

## In-lesson challenge
Problem: delivery notes keep showing wrong quantities. Why 1: the picker counted wrong. What is the best next why?

- A. Why was the picker careless?
  - Feedback: This points at the person and invites a guess about attitude. It is hard to check and will not change how the work is set up.
- B (correct). Why was it easy to miscount, for example, was the pick list unclear or the stock layout confusing?
  - Feedback: Yes. It turns the person-level answer into a question about the process, and each part can be checked by looking at the pick list and the shelves.
- C. Why do we not hire more pickers?
  - Feedback: This jumps to a solution before the cause is found. More people may not stop miscounts.

**Why:** A good next why explains the previous answer with something you can check, and it points at the process, not at the person.

## Questions after the lesson
### 1. A manager says, 'The root cause is that the packer was careless.' What is the best response?
- A. Agree, and coach the packer.
  - Feedback: Coaching may help, but 'careless' is a guess about a person, and it leaves the process unchanged.
- B (correct). Ask what in the process made a careless moment possible or likely, and check it.
  - Feedback: Right. It turns a person-level answer into a question about the process that you can check.
- C. Stop, because five whys has been used.
  - Feedback: The number five is a guide. The test is whether you have reached a cause the process lets you change.

**Why:** A person-level answer is usually the start of the next why, not the end.

### 2. Why were the labels late? Which answer is best?
- A. Because the labelling team is slow.
  - Feedback: That is an opinion about people, and it cannot easily be checked.
- B (correct). Because label printing starts only after the final quantity is confirmed in the system, which the timestamps show.
  - Feedback: Yes. It is a fact that can be checked, and it explains the earlier answer.
- C. Because Mondays are busy.
  - Feedback: That is vague, and it does not explain why these orders were late.

**Why:** Each answer should be a checkable fact that explains the one before it.

### 3. When is the five whys, on its own, not enough?
- A. Never. It always finds the one true cause.
  - Feedback: It finds a cause you can act on, not always the only one.
- B. Only when there are fewer than five answers.
  - Feedback: The number of answers is not the test.
- C (correct). When several causes interact or the facts are not known. Then use data and other tools alongside it.
  - Feedback: Right. Check with data, and use tools such as a Pareto chart or a fishbone to look at more than one chain.

**Why:** It works best on fairly direct chains of causes; complex problems need evidence and other tools too.
